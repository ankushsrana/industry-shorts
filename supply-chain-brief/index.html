Supply chains have more data and software than ever, yet the distance between a plan, a decision and an action on the ground is still where value leaks. Each story below looks at one part of that gap, using research from Gartner, McKinsey, BCG, KPMG, the World Economic Forum and others.

STORY 01 · AGENTIC AI

The $53 billion bet on supply chain agents

Spending is about to multiply. Proof of value has to keep up.

Gartner expects spending on supply chain management software with agentic AI capabilities to grow from less than $2 billion in 2025 to $53 billion by 2030. It also expects the share of enterprises using SCM software that have adopted agentic AI features to rise from 5% to 60% over the same period.1

The momentum 85%

Plan to raise AI spending in 2026

Accenture's research finds 30% of supply chain executives testing AI agents and 24% deploying them.15 KPMG finds 78% of large US companies expect at least moderate supply chain autonomy by 2027.12

The discipline >40%

Of agentic AI projects may be cancelled by 2027

Gartner cites cost, unclear business value and weak risk controls, and estimates only about 130 of the thousands of vendors claiming "agentic" capabilities are genuine. This is a cross-industry prediction.2

Gartner's advice is practical: invest first in clusters of simple agents that orchestrate multi-step workflows, and decide the right level of human-in-the-loop oversight, especially early on. Its 2026 technology trends place agentic AI and multiagent systems next to decision governance, a signal that autonomy and accountability are expected to arrive together.14

My take

The headline number is spend, not value. Agents that sit on top of weak data and unclear decision rights will be the first projects cancelled. The winners will start with narrow, repeatable decisions, such as replenishment exceptions, expedite calls and supplier follow-ups, then widen the guardrails as trust builds.

STORY 02 · PLANNING

Why planning systems still aren't paying back

The plan-to-execution gap, in three bars

Most large companies have already bought planning technology. BCG finds more than 70% have invested in an advanced planning system, yet only about one in five report meaningful value from advanced capabilities, and just 7% report value from agentic or generative AI.10

McKinsey's 2024 survey explains why. Only 10% had finished advanced planning deployments, a third lacked a quantified business case, 15% said implementations missed their objectives, and none rated their data as perfect.9 Meanwhile, responding to a disruption took two weeks on average. BCG puts it plainly: fully autonomous planning "remains largely aspirational", and planners are shifting from consolidating data to orchestrating decisions.11

“Ultimately, the constraint isn't technology.”

BCG, State of Supply Chain Planning 2025
My take

The next wave of value will not come from replacing planning systems. It will come from connecting them to execution: one loop where a signal becomes a recommendation, a recommendation becomes an approved action, and the outcome feeds the next plan.

STORY 03 · RISK

Tariffs exposed the tier-two blind spot

Risk is a daily operating condition, not an annual review

In McKinsey's 2025 risk pulse, 82% of supply chain leaders said new tariffs affect their supply chains, typically touching 20% to 40% of supply chain activity. On a weighted average, companies expected to pass on only 45% of the cost, so most of the rest has to be absorbed or engineered out.8

How companies are responding

The backdrop is unlikely to calm. The World Economic Forum ranks geoeconomic confrontation as the top global risk for 2026,14 and Accenture finds 76% of supply chain executives expect continued higher levels of disruption in 2026.15

My take

Tariff shocks turned risk management from a compliance exercise into a pricing, sourcing and inventory decision that changes weekly. That favours continuous, multi-tier risk sensing that is wired straight into planning, rather than a quarterly heat map.

STORY 04 · DATA & ANALYTICS

Analytics before agents: the data problem

Ambition versus reality, side by side

Three quarters of supply chain leaders in McKinsey's 2025 pulse are planning, blueprinting or piloting AI use cases, but only 19% say they are deploying AI tools at scale. Planned major investments in digital supply chain systems fell from 47% to 25% in a year.8

63%

Not sure their data is AI-ready

Organisations that lack, or are unsure they have, the right data management practices for AI.7

60%

Of AI projects at risk

Gartner predicts that, through 2026, organisations will abandon 60% of AI projects not supported by AI-ready data.7

83%

Scale AI step by step

Senior supply chain leaders who apply AI to specific use cases or scale it gradually, versus 17% pursuing immediate redesign.6

The use cases leading the way are the analytical core of supply chains: McKinsey names demand forecasting, inventory optimisation and supply planning as the top generative AI applications,8 and Gartner expects 70% of large organisations to adopt AI-based supply chain forecasting by 2030.5

My take

Analytics is not the warm-up act for AI; it is the foundation. Clean master data, shared definitions and explainable forecasts are what let an agent act without a human re-checking every number.

STORY 05 · TALENT

The talent pyramid is being redrawn

Agents change who does the work, and how people learn it

Gartner surveyed 509 supply chain leaders and found that changes in ways of working driven by AI and agentic AI are the single most influential driver of supply chain strategy over the next two years.3

High performers report higher agentic AI adoption across procurement, production, logistics, warehousing and planning, yet they prioritise reinventing their talent strategy over cutting headcount. In Gartner's words, they do not treat AI as "a blunt instrument for headcount reduction".3

My take

If agents absorb the routine tasks that used to train junior planners, companies must design a new apprenticeship: simulation, exception handling and decision reviews. The planner of 2030 is an orchestrator of decisions, not a spreadsheet operator.

STORY 06 · INDIA

India: from logistics cost to resilience

The efficiency story is maturing

India's logistics cost has been measured in different ways, and the estimates differ widely. The direction of travel is clearer than any single level.

2021–22 7.8–8.9%

of GDP

The same study's latest estimate, showing a decline.16

2020 report ~14%

of GDP

An earlier Arthur D Little and CII estimate, using a different method.17

My take

As basic logistics efficiency improves, Indian enterprises compete less on moving goods cheaply and more on planning quality, service and resilience. That is where decision intelligence, rather than transport-cost tools alone, starts to pay off, especially for Indian companies running global networks.
