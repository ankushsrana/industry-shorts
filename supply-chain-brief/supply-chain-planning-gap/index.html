I.

Volatility Is the Operating Condition

Disruption is no longer the exception

Large enterprises no longer lack planning software. They lack a reliable path from a plan to a decision, and from a decision to action on the ground. That gap matters more every year, because the environment those plans operate in has stopped settling down.

Three signals of a structural shift

#1

Geoeconomic confrontation

Ranked the top global risk for 2026 by more than 1,300 leaders and experts. Half expect a turbulent or stormy two years, up 14 points.9

2 wks

Average response time

90% of supply executives faced supply chain challenges in 2024, and planning and executing a response took two weeks on average.3

70%

Demand volatility

The top external challenge for planning leaders, ahead of trade and geopolitics (64%) and supply volatility (62%).1

Executives expect more of the same. Accenture's research reports that 76% of supply chain executives expect continued higher levels of disruption in 2026, and their self-rated readiness is lowest for geopolitical (38%) and environmental (34%) shocks.10

Our analysis

A two-week response cycle was tolerable when disruptions were rare. When most organisations face disruption every year, the response cycle itself becomes the competitive variable. Speed from signal to decision is now a buying criterion, not a feature.

II.

Investment Is Broad; Value Is Narrow

Why planning systems are not paying back

BCG finds that more than 70% of surveyed companies have invested in an advanced planning system, yet few consider themselves best-in-class. About one in five report meaningful value from advanced capabilities, and only 7% report value from agentic or generative AI.1

With more than 70% of enterprises already invested in planning systems, the opportunity is less about replacing them and more about making them pay back: cleaner data, connected decisions, and a business case measured from day one.

III.

Risk Visibility Is Eroding

Where disruptions actually start

Companies say risk is their top priority, yet the data points the other way. Tier-one visibility is improving, but deeper visibility, routine reporting and inventory buffers are all slipping, even though “major disruptions often start deep in the supply chain.”3

Four risk signals in the data

Click a card to open its file.

All four signals: McKinsey Global Supply Chain Leader Survey, 2024 3

Executives are responding. KPMG's 2026 survey of 462 US leaders at companies with $1 billion or more in revenue finds 94% innovating in risk and resilience now or within three years. Managing risk and geopolitical uncertainty is the top transformation objective, and cyber security is the top perceived risk, followed by multi-tier supplier exposure, shortages and regulatory pressure.11

Our analysis

The gap is between intent and capability. Risk intelligence that is continuous, multi-tier and tied directly to a recommended action closes it better than periodic reporting does.

IV.

Autonomy Is Arriving, With Discipline

The promise and the failure rate of agentic AI

Gartner predicts that 70% of large organisations will adopt AI-based forecasting by 2030.5 Its 2026 trends list places agentic AI, multiagent systems and decision governance side by side, a signal that autonomy and accountability are expected to arrive together.7

The promise 15%

of daily work decisions autonomous by 2028

Up from 0% in 2024, with 33% of enterprise software applications expected to include agentic AI. Figures cover all industries.8

The discipline >40%

of agentic AI projects cancelled by 2027

Due to cost, unclear value or weak risk controls. Gartner estimates only about 130 of thousands of “agentic” vendors are genuine.8

Accenture reports that 85% of supply chain executives plan to increase AI spending in 2026; 30% are testing AI agents, 24% deploying them and 21% building them into enterprise strategy.10 BCG is more cautious: fully autonomous planning “remains largely aspirational,” and planners are shifting from consolidating data to orchestrating decisions.2

Our analysis · How autonomy will scale
  1. Recommendations, with humans making the call.
  2. Approved actions, executed within agreed guardrails.
  3. Autonomous action, in narrow and well-governed domains.

Agentic capability depends on the planning and data layers beneath it. Governance, explainability and clear decision rights separate the projects that survive from those that are cancelled.

V.

What Enterprise Buyers Will Demand

Five criteria for the next three years

Read together, the evidence points to five criteria that will shape supply chain technology decisions. Open each one to see what supports it.

How the market is layered

Our framework, not a published ranking.

L1

Enterprise suites

Broad platforms that run core planning and records across the business.

L2

Planning specialists

Dedicated demand, supply and integrated business planning platforms.

L3

Visibility & execution

Networks that track shipments, suppliers and operations in near real time.

L4

AI-native decisioning

Newer tools that recommend, and increasingly take, decisions across layers.

Because most enterprises already run a planning system, a decision and execution layer that works alongside existing systems of record has a shorter path to value than one that asks the customer to start again.

VIII.

Outlook, 2027 to 2030

Five shifts to watch · our judgment, not a forecast

SHIFT 01

Planning and execution converge

A decision layer spanning plan, risk and execution becomes the reference architecture, on top of existing systems of record.

SHIFT 02

The agentic label gets tested

With only about 130 genuine agentic vendors by Gartner's estimate, buyers will ask for proof of governed action, not assistant demos.

SHIFT 03

Risk intelligence goes continuous

Periodic risk reporting gives way to multi-tier monitoring linked to recommended actions.

SHIFT 04

Value proof becomes a gate

Quantified business cases and time to first value move from sales claim to contract condition.

SHIFT 05

Planners orchestrate decisions

Talent gaps of 77% to 90% across surveys make augmentation a necessity rather than an option.

Summary

The main thread, in three parts

01 — The pressure is structural

  • Geoeconomic confrontation is the top global risk for 2026.
  • 90% of supply executives faced disruption in 2024.
  • A two-week response cycle is now a competitive weakness.

02 — Investment has outrun value

  • More than 70% have advanced planning systems; about one in five see meaningful value.
  • Data, talent and business cases are the real constraints.
  • Deep-tier risk visibility and routine reporting are slipping.

03 — Autonomy will be earned

  • Autonomy scales in steps: recommend, approve, then act within guardrails.
  • Over 40% of agentic projects may be cancelled for cost, value or control reasons.
  • Buyers will favour connected, governed platforms that prove value fast.
